What Does Black Mold Look Like?

Black mold is typically dark green to black, with a slimy or fuzzy texture and a distinct musty odor. But color alone can't confirm it's the toxic kind — here's how to identify it and what actually matters.

What Black Mold Looks Like

Black mold — most often referring to Stachybotrys chartarum — has a fairly recognizable appearance, though it's frequently confused with other dark molds and even ordinary dirt or soot.

  • Color: dark green to greenish-black, sometimes with a grayish tint. Rarely a pure "black."
  • Texture: often slimy or wet-looking when actively growing (it needs a lot of moisture), turning powdery and dry as it ages.
  • Pattern: tends to grow in circular or irregular clustered patches rather than thin streaks.
  • Sheen: a slightly shiny, almost greasy appearance when wet — a key differentiator from the flatter look of mildew.

Here's the honest truth most sites won't tell you: you cannot reliably identify toxic black mold by sight. Many harmless molds are also dark, and Stachybotrys can look like other species. The only way to know for certain is professional testing.

Where Black Mold Grows

Black mold needs sustained moisture — more than most molds — so it shows up where water has been present for days or longer:

  • Behind walls after a plumbing leak or flood
  • On drywall, wallpaper, and ceiling tiles
  • Around windows with chronic condensation
  • In bathrooms with poor ventilation
  • In basements, crawl spaces, and attics
  • On wood framing and subflooring after water damage
  • Inside HVAC systems and ductwork

A quick field test: dab the spot with a little diluted dish soap on a cloth. If it wipes away completely and doesn't return, it's likely dirt. If it stains, smears, or comes back, it's probably mold.

Is It Actually Dangerous?

The "toxic black mold" reputation is partly overblown and partly real. Here's the balanced truth: Stachybotrys can produce mycotoxins, and prolonged exposure is associated with respiratory symptoms, headaches, and irritation — particularly for people with asthma, allergies, or compromised immune systems. But many dark molds are far less concerning, and the sensational claims (like mold causing severe illness in healthy adults from brief exposure) aren't well supported.

The practical takeaway: any significant mold growth indicates a moisture problem that needs fixing, and larger areas should be handled professionally regardless of species. Don't panic — but don't ignore it either.

What to Do If You Find Black Mold

  • Don't disturb it. Scrubbing or scraping releases spores into the air.
  • Find and fix the moisture source — mold is a symptom; the water is the cause.
  • Small areas (under ~10 sq ft) on non-porous surfaces can sometimes be cleaned carefully with proper protection.
  • Larger areas, porous materials, or HVAC involvement need professional remediation with containment.
  • Consider professional testing if anyone in the home has unexplained respiratory symptoms.

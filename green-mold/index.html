Green Mold: Causes, Dangers & Removal

Green mold is one of the most common household molds — and "green" covers hundreds of species, including Penicillium, Aspergillus, and Cladosporium. Here's what it means and what to do.

What Green Mold Is

Green mold isn't one thing — it's a color shared by hundreds of fungal species. The most common green molds in homes are Penicillium, Aspergillus, and Cladosporium. They often appear fuzzy or powdery and range from bright green to dark olive.

You've seen green mold before: it's the fuzz on spoiled bread, rotting fruit, and old cheese. In your home, it grows on damp walls, wood, fabric, and food — anywhere with moisture and something organic to feed on.

What Causes Green Mold

  • Moisture — leaks, flooding, condensation, high humidity
  • Organic material — drywall, wood, fabric, paper, food
  • Poor ventilation — bathrooms, basements, closets
  • Damp building materials after water damage
  • Warm temperatures that accelerate growth

Is Green Mold Dangerous?

It depends on the species and your sensitivity. Some green molds are relatively benign; others — certain Aspergillus species in particular — can cause respiratory infections and allergic reactions, especially in people with asthma, allergies, or weakened immune systems.

Common symptoms of green mold exposure include sneezing, coughing, runny nose, watery eyes, throat irritation, and worsened asthma. As with all mold, the risk is higher with larger growth, longer exposure, and more sensitive individuals.

Because you can't identify the specific species by its green color, the safe approach is to treat any significant green mold growth as something to remove promptly and investigate for a moisture source.

How to Remove Green Mold

  • Small areas on non-porous surfaces: clean with detergent and water or a diluted bleach solution, wearing gloves and a mask
  • Fix the moisture source first — otherwise it returns
  • Porous materials (drywall, carpet, insulation) that are heavily affected usually need to be removed, not cleaned
  • Improve ventilation and control humidity (keep indoor humidity below 50%)
  • Larger areas or recurring growth need professional remediation with containment

When to Call a Professional

Handle it professionally when: the affected area is larger than about 10 square feet, the mold is on porous materials or inside walls, it keeps coming back after cleaning, it's in the HVAC system, or anyone in the home has unexplained respiratory symptoms. Professional remediation includes containment, HEPA filtration, safe removal, and — critically — identifying and fixing the moisture source so it doesn't regrow.
